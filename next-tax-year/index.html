Your next tax year: 2027/28

For someone paid by an employer and nothing else, take-home pay does not change from 6 April 2027. The announced changes reach savers, landlords, company car drivers, people with side income and parents. They are announced, not yet in an Act, so the Finance Bill may change them.

These are example people, not forecasts. Each shows 2026/27 against 2027/28, with every announced change applied.

Example people

  • Pay of £60,000 and nothing else: take-home pay is £45,357 a year in both years, so nothing changes.
  • Pay of £30,000 and £1,200 of savings interest: take-home pay would be about £8 a year less (£26,280 now, £26,272 from 6 April 2027), because of: Tax on savings interest.
  • Pay of £50,000 and £10,000 of rent from a property: take-home pay would be about £360 a year less (£45,974 now, £45,614 from 6 April 2027), because of: Tax on property income.
  • Pay of £60,000 and a company car with a list price of £40,000: take-home pay would be about £160 a year less (£44,717 now, £44,557 from 6 April 2027), because of: Company car tax.
  • Pay of £50,000 and £4,000 of side income: take-home pay would be about £800 a year more (£42,374 now, £43,174 from 6 April 2027), because of: Trading allowance.
  • Pay of £50,000 and two children under 11: Child Benefit would be about £83 a year more, because of: Child Benefit rates.

Based on announced rules. The Finance Bill may change them. Income Tax thresholds are frozen until 5 April 2031, which is already law.
